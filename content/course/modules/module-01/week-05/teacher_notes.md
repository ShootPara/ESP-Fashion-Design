# Teacher Notes: Week 5 - Body, Figure, and Observation Drawing

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may confuse body-reference points that are close together, especially `neck` and `shoulder`, or `waist` and `hip`. They may also mix Week 4 garment-part language with Week 5 figure language, for example using `waistband` when the target word is `waist`. Another common problem is grammar: `The figure standing`, `The figure is using a dress`, `This is front view`, or `The silhouette is simple long`.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the body-reference diagram and the four-figure gallery visible throughout the week. Students should not have to imagine the figure without image support at this stage. Move from recognition to production in a steady sequence: review, match, label, listen, read, complete frames, then write and speak. During the Fashion Figure Description Card, encourage students to complete one line at a time rather than trying to write a paragraph.

## Professional Context Notes
Keep the language connected to observation drawing, croquis work, and design description. Students are learning how to identify body-reference points on a drawn figure, name the pose and the view, and describe how the garment looks on the figure. The week should sound like beginner Fashion Design observation language, not like general appearance commentary.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Some learners may already know the word `croquis` from Fashion Design classes, while others may prefer `sketch`. Present both clearly and keep `croquis` tied to fashion-drawing context. Also remind students that `silhouette` and `proportion` are common design words, but Week 5 uses them lightly and observationally rather than technically.

## Simplify This Week
Reduce the Fashion Figure Description Card to exactly five required lines:
- This is a fashion figure / sketch / croquis.
- The figure is standing / walking / sitting.
- This is a front view / side view / back view.
- The figure is wearing ___.
- The silhouette is ___.

Accept one visible detail sentence only if needed: `The garment has ___`. For Activity 3, keep only six required point labels. In the speaking task, accept 3 to 4 short spoken sentences with strong teacher prompting if needed.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to use the optional challenge line with `outline`, `body line`, or a short preference reason. During the teacher session, invite a follow-up sentence about why the pose is clear, why the silhouette is simple, or how the garment shape works on the figure.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, vocabulary matching, labeling task, listening task, reading task, phrase-practice task, and correction task can all be app-checked with immediate feedback. The Fashion Figure Description Card should remain teacher-visible because it is the main written artifact and benefits from a small revision cycle. The speaking checkpoint should remain teacher-observed and non-recorded, with the app serving only to display the image gallery, sentence frames, and completion notes.
