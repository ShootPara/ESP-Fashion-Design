# Week 2: The Fashion Studio

## Overview
Week 2 introduces the fashion studio as the student’s first practical English environment inside Fashion Design study. The week stays highly visual and A2-accessible. Students begin with a studio-scene review, learn core tool and material vocabulary, read a detailed image, listen to simple instructions, practice tool-and-purpose phrases, write a short structured studio-kit text, and review one sentence correction before a teacher-led speaking session.

Week 2 follows the same general rhythm as Week 1, but it shifts the content from identity language to concrete objects, places, and actions. The priority is recognition before production, then short controlled production before live speaking.

## Learning Objectives
- Recognize and name common Fashion Design studio tools, materials, and work areas.
- Understand short A2-level studio instructions with simple classroom actions.
- Identify objects and location phrases in a visual studio scene.
- Use sentence frames such as `This is ___`, `These are ___`, `I use ___ to ___`, and `I need ___`.
- Write a short 4-5 sentence studio-kit text with strong support, with one extra sentence only as an optional challenge.
- Revise one sentence focused on articles, plurals, or missing `to`.
- Say 4-6 short sentences about tools and purposes during the teacher session.

## Professional Fashion-Design Context
This week is closely tied to introductory sewing, patternmaking, observation drawing, and creativity lab routines. Students need English not only for abstract study goals but also for everyday classroom actions: taking a sketchbook, measuring fabric, finding tools on the table, and naming what they use in the studio. The tasks reflect the language of materials, surfaces, tools, and teacher instructions rather than generic ESL vocabulary.

## Language Objectives
- Name common objects such as `scissors`, `ruler`, `measuring tape`, `needle`, `thread`, `pins`, `sewing machine`, `mannequin`, `sketchbook`, `pattern paper`, and `fabric swatch`.
- Understand and use short imperative verbs such as `take`, `put`, `draw`, `cut`, `measure`, `fold`, and `pin`.
- Use singular and plural object phrases more accurately, especially `a ruler`, `a pencil`, `scissors`, and `pins`.
- Connect object and purpose with `I use ___ to ___`.
- Build a short text from sentence frames instead of writing a broad paragraph from scratch.

## Materials Needed
- One full studio or worktable scene for the opener and visual-reading task.
- One visual vocabulary board or small set of isolated tool and material images.
- One short studio-instructions audio clip or teacher read-aloud fallback.
- Sentence frames for `I use ___ to ___`, `I need ___`, and `My favorite tool is ___`.
- Student writing space for the Week 2 studio-kit text.
- Optional printed image cards for the teacher-led naming and speaking session.

## Suggested Sequence
1. `m01w02-a01` Welcome Back: students review familiar words and notice a studio scene.
2. `m01w02-a02` Studio Vocabulary: students match images, words, and Portuguese support for tools and materials.
3. `m01w02-a03` Look Around the Studio: students answer object and location questions from a detailed image.
4. `m01w02-a04` Listen: Studio Instructions: students hear short teacher directions and choose tools, actions, and order.
5. `m01w02-a05` Tool + Action Phrase Practice: students complete `I use ___ to ___` phrases.
6. `m01w02-a06` My Studio Kit: students build a short structured text from sentence frames.
7. `m01w02-a07` Review and Improve: students correct one sentence with an article, plural, or infinitive problem.
8. `m01w02-a08` Teacher Session: students name studio items and explain what they use them for in live speaking.

## Differentiation Options
- Keep image support visible during vocabulary, listening, and writing.
- Let learners who need more support choose from a reduced word bank before typing sentence frames.
- Provide a model tool-and-action phrase beside the writing task for students who need stronger scaffolding.
- Allow pair rehearsal before the teacher-observed speaking task.
- Ask stronger learners to add one extra sentence about location or usefulness, such as `The fabric is on the table` or `My sketchbook is useful because I draw ideas in class`.

## Extension Options
- Invite stronger learners to sort tools into drawing, measuring, and sewing categories using English labels.
- Ask students to add one more sentence comparing two tools, for example `I use a pencil to draw, but I use pins to hold fabric`.
- During the teacher session, ask confident learners one follow-up question such as `Where is your measuring tape?` or `Why is your sketchbook useful?`

## Notes for Future App Implementation
Week 2 maps cleanly to a visual-first mobile flow. The app-facing design should preserve:

- a reusable studio-scene image that supports both recognition and location tasks
- image-word matching with Portuguese support hidden behind the target English focus
- audio replay for the studio-instructions task with transcript reveal only after an attempt
- sentence-frame writing fields rather than one large blank text box
- a teacher-visible structured writing submission with revision support for the studio-kit task
- a teacher-observed speaking checkpoint that tracks completion without recording audio

The week is especially reusable because the vocabulary boards and the `I use ___ to ___` pattern will return in later modules.
