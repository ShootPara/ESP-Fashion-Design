# Teacher Notes: Week 2 - The Fashion Studio

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may confuse similar tools such as ruler and measuring tape, or thread and fabric. They may omit articles in singular phrases like `I use ruler` or `I have pencil`. They may also overgeneralize plurals, for example `a scissors`, or produce incomplete purpose phrases such as `I use scissors cut fabric`. In listening, learners may identify familiar objects but miss the order of instructions.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the week highly visual and concrete. Show the image before explaining too much language. Model one item or one phrase first, then let students complete the rest. During the teacher session, allow students to look at their Week 2 writing while they speak. Keep the correction step narrow so students act on one clear grammar point rather than trying to fix everything at once.

## Professional Context Notes
Use real Fashion Design routines and avoid generic school-only examples. Point back to drawing, sewing, patternmaking, and classroom studio organization. When students say what they use, encourage answers that sound like real studio practice: `I use a measuring tape to measure`, `I use pins to hold fabric`, and `I use a sketchbook to draw ideas`.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Some terms may overlap with words students already know from Portuguese or from informal fashion media, but the classroom goal is accurate English use in a studio-learning context. Keep the tone adult and practical. A2 language should still sound connected to university study and future professional work.

## Simplify This Week
Reduce the writing task to four required frames only:
- In my studio kit, I have ___.
- I use ___ to ___.
- I need ___ for ___.
- My favorite tool is ___.

For listening, allow students to look at a small image word bank before the second play. For the teacher session, accept short 3-4 sentence speaking with notes if needed.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to add one location sentence such as `The scissors are on the table` or one usefulness sentence such as `My sketchbook is useful because I draw ideas in it`. In the teacher session, they can answer a follow-up question about where an item is or why it matters in class.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, vocabulary matching, visual reading, listening, phrase practice, and correction task can all be auto-checked or self-corrected in a future app. The My Studio Kit task should remain teacher-visible because it is the week’s meaningful writing output and includes revision support. The teacher-led speaking task should remain a non-recorded, teacher-observed checkpoint with sentence frames, a pronunciation focus list, and completion notes.
