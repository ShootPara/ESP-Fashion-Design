# Teacher Notes: Week 8 - Sewing Actions

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may confuse tool words with action words, especially `pins` versus `pin`, `iron` versus `press`, and `line` as a drawn line versus thread. They may also forget `to` in `Use ___ to ___`, leave out articles in phrases such as `fold the edge`, or lose the sequence order when they try to produce several steps at once. Another common difficulty is writing a list of words instead of complete imperative sentences.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the Week 8 process boards visible throughout the week. Students should be able to point to a step while they answer. Move steadily from recognition to production: opener, action matching, tool phrases, reading, listening, phrase practice, structured writing, correction, then speaking. During the writing task, encourage students to complete one line at a time instead of trying to write a paragraph or tell the whole process from memory.

## Professional Context Notes
Keep the week connected to beginner sewing workflow, small classroom samples, and clear studio actions. Students are not doing advanced garment-construction explanation yet. They are learning how to say what happens first, what tool is used, where the action happens, and how to check the result. This language is useful later for patternmaking, prototyping, simple tutorials, and technical-process description.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Some learners may already know Portuguese sewing vocabulary very well but may not have clear English equivalents for simple classroom instructions. Keep the English target practical and high-frequency. Also explain that `right side` and `wrong side` refer to the sides of the fabric, not to moral ideas of right and wrong.

## Simplify This Week
Keep the writing card at exactly six required lines:
- `First, ___.`
- `Then, ___.`
- `Next, ___.`
- `After that, ___.`
- `Use ___ to ___.`
- `Finally, check ___.`

Use the shared `image A` to `image E` gallery as a visual reference, not as a scored choice. Accept one optional challenge sentence only if the student is ready: `This step is important because ___.` Mark the writing card by checking the 6 required lines, the sequence order, the tool phrase, and the final check line. In the speaking task, accept 5 short spoken steps with strong teacher prompting if needed.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to add the optional challenge sentence with a clear process reason. During the teacher session, invite one follow-up sentence about quality such as `The line should be straight` or about tool choice such as `Use an iron to press the hem.`

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, sewing-actions matching, tool-action phrase practice, reading task, listening task, phrase-practice task, and correction task can all be app-checked with immediate feedback. The Simple Sewing Process Card should remain teacher-visible because it is the main written artifact, uses 6 required lines plus 1 optional challenge sentence, and benefits from a small revision cycle. The speaking checkpoint should remain teacher-observed and non-recorded, with the app serving only to display the shared process gallery, sentence frames, and completion notes.
