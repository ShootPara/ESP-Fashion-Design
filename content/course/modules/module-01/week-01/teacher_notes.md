# Teacher Notes: Week 1 - Welcome to Fashion Design English

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may omit the verb in "I am," forget the article in "a fashion design student," or use Portuguese word order in short English sentences. Many learners will also confuse course areas and design objects, for example using sketch, drawing, garment, and fabric interchangeably. In listening, they may recognize isolated words but miss the speaker's overall goal. In writing, students may produce very general statements unless the teacher keeps the Fashion Design context visible.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep Week 1 low-pressure and welcoming. Model each task briefly before students work independently. During the teacher-led session, allow students to read from their written profile first, then encourage more natural delivery on a second try. Use quick checks rather than long explanations, especially during pronunciation work and profile sharing.

## Professional Context Notes
Students should leave Week 1 understanding that English is a practical tool for Fashion Design study, not a separate abstract subject. Refer often to real first-semester work such as drawing classes, sewing, patternmaking, creativity lab, text production, studio routines, and early portfolio writing. When students name their interests, invite answers that sound realistic for a design student rather than generic hobbies.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Some students may already know fashion vocabulary from media, trends, or brand culture, but they may not yet know how to use those words in an academic or studio setting. Keep examples adult and professionally grounded. Avoid making Week 1 feel childish just because the language level is controlled.

## Simplify This Week
Reduce the profile task to four required sentences if needed:
- My name is ___.
- I am a fashion design student.
- I like ___.
- I want to learn English for ___.

If the listening task feels too demanding, read the script aloud slowly, pause between ideas, and allow students to check a keyword list before the second listen.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to add a sentence about a future design interest such as sustainability, accessories, styling, or fashion research. In the teacher-led session, they can also answer a follow-up question and give one simple feedback phrase to a classmate, such as "I like your idea about sketches" or "You can add one more detail."

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
In a future app, the reading, listening, and vocabulary activities can be auto-checked, while the writing task should remain teacher-visible with light rubric guidance. The correction-review task should save the revised sentence so teachers can see whether the learner acted on feedback. The teacher-led session should remain represented in the canonical week content even if it is not app-graded, because it is where pronunciation, confidence, and live support are reinforced.
