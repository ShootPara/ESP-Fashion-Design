# Teacher Notes: Week 1 — Welcome to Fashion Design English

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may omit the verb in "I am," drop the article in "a Fashion Design student," or use Portuguese word order when writing preferences and goals. Pronunciation may also need support for "fashion," "design," "sketch," and "portfolio."

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the tone welcoming and low-pressure. Model one short introduction before asking students to write. During the teacher-led session, let students read from their profile if needed before inviting more natural delivery.

## Professional Context Notes
This week should feel relevant to real Fashion Design study from the start. When possible, refer to drawing, sewing, patternmaking, creativity, and portfolio language so students see English as a practical studio tool rather than a separate subject.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Students may know some fashion words through media, social platforms, or brand culture, but they may not yet distinguish between casual fashion vocabulary and clearer classroom or portfolio language. Keep examples adult, practical, and beginner-accessible.

## Simplify This Week
If students need more support, reduce the writing target to four guided sentences and allow a stronger Portuguese scaffold during instructions. The listening text can also be read aloud slowly by the teacher instead of using an audio file.

## Extend This Week
Ask confident learners to add one sentence about a future design area, such as accessories, sustainability, or styling. They can also ask and answer one follow-up question with a partner during the teacher-led session.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
In a future app, the reading and listening tasks can be auto-checked, while the guided profile can be teacher-reviewed with lightweight rubric support. The oral introduction can remain teacher-observed or become an optional audio submission in a later phase.
