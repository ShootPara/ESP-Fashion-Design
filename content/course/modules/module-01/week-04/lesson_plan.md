# Week 4: Parts of a Garment

## Overview
Week 4 builds directly from Week 3. Students already know common garment names and categories, so the new focus is on the visible parts and details of garments. The week stays visual, concrete, and A2-first. Students begin with a quick review of whole garments, learn a core garment-part vocabulary set, label one garment image, listen to short detail descriptions, practice reliable phrase frames, build a scaffolded Garment Details Card, review one correction target, and explain one garment in a teacher-supported speaking session.

The main artifact is not a paragraph. Instead, students complete a short structured Garment Details Card with required sentence-frame lines. This keeps the writing useful and professional without asking A2 learners for broad open production.

## Learning Objectives
- Recognize and name common visible garment parts in English.
- Match garment-part images to English vocabulary with Portuguese support when needed.
- Label visible details on a garment image using clear app-checkable choices.
- Understand short spoken descriptions of garment parts and locations.
- Use sentence frames such as `It has ___`, `There is a ___ on the front`, `There are ___ on the front`, and `The ___ is at the bottom`.
- Complete a short Garment Details Card using 5 to 6 required sentence-frame lines and one optional challenge line.
- Revise one sentence focused on singular and plural detail nouns or location phrases.
- Explain one garment in 4 to 6 short spoken sentences during the teacher session.

## Professional Fashion-Design Context
Garment-part vocabulary helps Fashion Design students move from naming a garment to observing and describing how it is made and how it looks. Students need this language for drawing, sewing, beginner patternmaking, technical observation, simple critique, and future portfolio captions.

Week 4 is especially useful because it introduces the language of visible details in an adult, professional context. Students are not only saying what the garment is. They are also naming parts, describing where those parts are, and beginning to comment on whether a detail is simple, decorative, or functional.

## Language Objectives
- Name garment parts such as `collar`, `neckline`, `sleeve`, `cuff`, `shoulder`, `pocket`, `zipper`, `button`, `waistband`, `hem`, `seam`, and `lining`.
- Use construction-detail words such as `pleat`, `dart`, and `stitch` at a beginner-accessible recognition level.
- Use location phrases such as `on the front`, `on the back`, `at the top`, `at the bottom`, `on the side`, and `near the hem`.
- Use controlled description words such as `visible`, `simple`, `decorative`, `functional`, `loose`, and `fitted`.
- Build accurate A2 detail-description sentences with `It has`, `They have`, `There is`, and `There are`.
- Improve singular and plural detail language such as `a collar`, `two pockets`, `buttons`, and `long sleeves`.

## Materials Needed
- One garment-detail overview board for the opener.
- Two garment-parts vocabulary boards with close-up detail visuals.
- One single garment image with visible label points for the visual-reading task.
- One reusable garment card or gallery set for listening, writing, and speaking support.
- One short audio clip or teacher read-aloud fallback for the listening task.
- Visible sentence frames for the Week 4 language focus.
- Student writing space for the Garment Details Card.

## Suggested Sequence
1. `m01w04-a01` Welcome Back: students review garment names and notice visible details.
2. `m01w04-a02` Garment Parts Vocabulary: students match garment-part images to English words and then use a short Portuguese bridge as support.
3. `m01w04-a03` Label the Garment: students inspect one garment image and choose labels and location phrases for marked points.
4. `m01w04-a04` Listen: students hear four short garment descriptions and choose the correct garment, detail, or location answer.
5. `m01w04-a05` Phrase Practice: students complete controlled frames with `It has`, `There is`, `There are`, and location phrases.
6. `m01w04-a06` Garment Details Card: students choose one garment and complete a structured detail card with required sentence frames.
7. `m01w04-a07` Review and Improve: students correct one common Week 4 sentence problem.
8. `m01w04-a08` Teacher Session: students practice pronunciation, label details live, and explain one garment using their card.

## Differentiation Options
- Keep the Week 4 garment images visible during vocabulary, labeling, listening, and writing.
- Let learners who need more support complete fewer choices at first, then retry with the full set.
- Provide a reduced word bank for the Garment Details Card if students need stronger support.
- Allow students to choose between a singular garment and a plural garment based on confidence.
- During speaking, allow learners to point to details on an image card while they speak.

## Extension Options
- Ask stronger learners to use the optional challenge line with `functional` or `decorative`, or to add one more sentence using `There are ___ near the ___`.
- Invite stronger learners to compare two garments with one short contrast sentence.
- In the teacher session, ask confident learners one follow-up question about where a detail is or why it is useful.

## Notes for Future App Implementation
Week 4 should stay image-first and detail-focused in a future app. The app-facing flow should preserve:

- one overview board for fast visual review
- two main image-word matching sets or boards as the primary saved vocabulary output
- one labeled garment task with explicit point keys and answer choices
- one listening task whose audio script matches the answer key exactly
- one phrase-practice task centered on fill-blank sentence frames, not mixed ambiguous outputs
- one teacher-reviewed structured writing card with separate sentence-frame fields instead of a blank paragraph box
- one teacher-observed speaking checkpoint that displays prompts and tracks completion without recording audio

The Week 4 garment-part set should stay reusable in later weeks about drawing, construction, fabric, technical description, and simple critique.
