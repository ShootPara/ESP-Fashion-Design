# Teacher Notes: Week 4 - Parts of a Garment

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may confuse closely related words such as `collar` and `neckline`, or `seam` and `stitch`. They may also know the Portuguese idea but not understand which English word is used for the visible part and which word is used for the construction process. Another common problem is grammar: students may write `It has two pocket`, `There is buttons`, or `The hem is in the bottom` instead of the target Week 4 patterns.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the image support visible throughout the week. Students should not have to remember the detail vocabulary without visual reference at this stage. Move from recognition to production in a steady sequence: identify, label, listen, practice sentence frames, then write and speak. During the Garment Details Card, encourage students to complete one line at a time instead of trying to write freely.

## Professional Context Notes
Keep the week connected to real Fashion Design observation. Students are learning language for visible details they may draw, sew, label, describe, or discuss in class. This is not only clothing vocabulary. It is beginner technical language for talking about garment structure and visible design details.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Some learners may already know fashion loanwords such as `zipper`, `lining`, or `fitted`, while other terms like `dart` and `pleat` may be new even in Portuguese-supported form. Treat those more technical words as image-supported recognition items first. Also remind students that some detail words are easier to understand when they are attached to one garment image instead of taught as abstract definitions.

## Simplify This Week
Reduce the Garment Details Card to exactly five required lines:
- This is a ___ / These are ___.
- It has ___ / They have ___.
- There is a ___ on the ___.
- The ___ is at the ___.
- I like this detail because ___.

For Activity 3, reduce the label set to six points instead of eight. In the speaking task, accept 3 to 4 short spoken sentences with strong teacher prompting if needed.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to use the optional challenge line with `functional` or `decorative`, or to add one more sentence using `There are ___ near the ___`. During the teacher session, invite a short follow-up question about whether a detail is useful, simple, or more decorative.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, vocabulary matching, labeling task, listening task, phrase practice, and correction task can all be app-checked with immediate feedback. The Garment Details Card should remain teacher-visible because it is the main written artifact and benefits from a small revision cycle. The speaking checkpoint should remain teacher-observed and non-recorded, with the app serving only to display image cards, sentence frames, and completion notes.
