# Teacher Notes: Week 7 - Fabric and Texture Basics

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may confuse what the fabric feels like with what it looks like, especially with pairs such as `soft` versus `shiny` and `rough` versus `matte`. They may also forget `of` in `made of`, treat fabric nouns like countable items in simple sentences, or choose too many words at once without forming a full sentence. Another common difficulty is using support-tier words such as `breathable` or `textile` before they are comfortable with the core Week 7 set.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the Week 7 boards and galleries visible throughout the week. Students should be able to point to a swatch while they answer. Move steadily from recognition to production: opener, material matching, quality matching, reading, listening, phrase practice, structured writing, correction, then speaking. During the writing task, encourage students to complete one line at a time instead of trying to write a paragraph.

## Professional Context Notes
Keep the week connected to swatches, samples, simple fabric choice, and beginner sewing or garment-planning decisions. Students are not doing advanced textile science. They are learning how to say what a material is, how it feels, how it looks, and what garment or weather use it suits. This language is useful later for textiles, sustainability, product description, collection planning, and styling discussions.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Many learners may already know a few fashion or retail words such as `denim`, `lace`, or `cropped`, but they may not know how to place them in clear English frames. Keep the focus on accurate, simple usage rather than on sounding advanced. Also remind students that some fabric words remain uncountable in simple use, as in `This is cotton` rather than `a cotton`.

## Simplify This Week
Keep the writing card at exactly six required lines:
- `This is ...` or `This is a ... fabric swatch.`
- `It is ...`
- `It feels ...`
- `It looks ...`
- `It is ...`
- `It is good for ...` or `It can be used for ...`

Accept one optional challenge sentence only if the student is ready: `I like this fabric because ...`. In the speaking task, accept 4 short spoken sentences with strong teacher prompting if needed.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to use the optional challenge sentence with a clear reason linked to garment type, comfort, or season. During the teacher session, invite one follow-up sentence comparing two fabrics or explaining why a fabric suits a silhouette.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, materials matching, qualities matching, reading task, listening task, phrase-practice task, and correction task can all be app-checked with immediate feedback. The Fabric Swatch Description Card should remain teacher-visible because it is the main written artifact, uses 6 required lines plus 1 optional challenge sentence, and benefits from a small revision cycle. The speaking checkpoint should remain teacher-observed and non-recorded, with the app serving only to display the gallery, sentence frames, and completion notes.
