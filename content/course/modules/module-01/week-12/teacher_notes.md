# Teacher Notes: Week 12 - Creative Inspiration

## Common Learner Difficulties
- Students may name a visible detail but not explain where the idea comes from.
- Students may confuse source words such as reference, theme, and concept.
- Students may use inspired in instead of inspired by or write because without a full reason clause.
- Mood words can become vague unless the visual cue is strong and the answer choices are limited.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
- Keep the image keys visible on the board while students answer app-checked tasks.
- Pause before the listening task and remind students to scan image A to image D first.
- During the writing task, coach students to complete one line at a time instead of drafting a paragraph.
- Keep the speaking task short and scaffolded by letting students use the saved card as support.

## Professional Context Notes
Fashion students regularly explain where an idea comes from when discussing moodboards, sketches, concept directions, and early portfolio captions. This week supports that professional habit by linking inspiration language to specific visible choices and simple rationale.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Reference in this week means a design reference, not a bibliographic citation. Mood should be taught as atmosphere or impression, not only personal emotion. Movement refers to visible motion or flow in the design, not only physical exercise.

## Simplify This Week
- Use only four core sources during the first pass: nature, city, music, and architecture.
- Model the rationale frames with visible sentence starters before asking students to write.
- Accept short noun phrases in early practice before asking for full because clauses in the writing card.

## Extend This Week
- Ask students to add one extra effect sentence with This creates ... or This makes the look ....
- Invite students to compare two gallery images using simple contrast language.
- Ask stronger students to explain how one source affects both color and silhouette.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The asynchronous flow works well if the stable image keys remain visible and the app saves exact selections, matches, fill blanks, and structured lines. For teacher review, show the selected image key, the six required writing lines, the optional challenge line, the checklist, common errors, and feedback prompts. The speaking task should remain teacher-observed only with no recording or upload step.
