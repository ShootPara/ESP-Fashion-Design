# Week 12: Creative Inspiration

## Overview
Week 12 moves from visible description into simple design rationale. Students learn to name common inspiration sources, connect them to visible design choices, and describe the mood or effect a design creates. The week ends with one scaffolded inspiration-note card and one short teacher-supported speaking explanation.

## Learning Objectives
- Identify common inspiration sources such as nature, city, music, art, architecture, history, memory, and movement.
- Use short rationale frames such as This design is inspired by ..., The colors come from ..., and I chose ... because ....
- Recognize simple mood and effect language such as calm, bold, playful, elegant, and modern.
- Read and listen to short inspiration notes tied to stable image keys.
- Produce one structured Design Inspiration Note Card with six required short lines.

## Professional Fashion-Design Context
Students use the kind of English they need for early concept development, inspiration-board discussion, sketch rationale, and short portfolio-ready explanation language. This week helps them move from naming visible features to explaining where a design idea comes from and why specific colors, fabrics, lines, or details were chosen.

## Language Objectives
- Name inspiration sources and simple themes.
- Connect a source to one visible design element.
- Use because to explain one design choice.
- Use The overall look is ... to describe a final impression.

## Materials Needed
- Week 12 opener board with one look and several inspiration thumbnails.
- Week 12 keyed inspiration-source board A to H.
- Week 12 keyed mood-and-effect board.
- Week 12 reusable image A to image D gallery.
- Week 12 listening clip aligned exactly to the reusable gallery.
- Optional projected or printed copies of the reusable gallery for the writing and speaking tasks.

## Suggested Sequence
1. Start with the opener so students move from detail observation to the idea of design inspiration.
2. Teach the core inspiration-source vocabulary through the keyed image board.
3. Use the mood-and-effect task to connect visible design cues to a likely impression.
4. Model short inspiration notes through reading before the listening task.
5. Use the listening task with the same stable gallery to reinforce the same source-to-design logic.
6. Consolidate the language with short phrase-practice fill blanks.
7. Move into the main structured writing card with one chosen gallery image.
8. End the online sequence with two narrow correction cards.
9. Use the teacher session for pronunciation support and one short spoken explanation.

## Timing Estimate
The student-facing weekly flow is designed for about 144 minutes total, including a 50-minute teacher-observed speaking session.

## Differentiation Options
- Keep Portuguese instructions visible while students produce English answers.
- Limit the first pass to four core inspiration sources: nature, city, music, and architecture.
- Let stronger students use the optional challenge line in the writing card.
- During the speaking task, allow students to read from the saved card before speaking more freely.

## Extension Options
- Ask students to compare two inspiration sources from the gallery using simple contrast language.
- Ask students to add one extra sentence with This creates ... or This makes the look ....
- Reuse the Week 12 gallery as a warm-up when moving into Week 13 feedback language.

## Notes for Future App Implementation
The app should keep stable visible keys on the screen whenever a task depends on them. The reusable image A to image D gallery should support the reading, listening, writing, and speaking tasks without changing key assignments. The writing card should save the chosen image key plus each required line separately. The listening task should allow replay and reveal the script only after the first completed attempt. The teacher review flow should show the selected image key, the saved structured lines, the checklist, common errors, and return-for-revision support for the writing task only.
