# Teacher Notes: Week 11 — Design Details

## Common Learner Difficulties
- Students may confuse the name of the detail with the garment part around it, such as neckline versus embroidery.
- Students may overuse pocket or zipper and avoid less familiar words such as pleat, ruffle, or topstitching.
- There is and There are may need quick review when students describe one detail versus two details.
- Students may say in the front instead of on the front, or use decorative when they mean functional.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
- Keep the image keys visible on the board while students answer app-checked tasks.
- Pause before the listening task and remind students to scan image A to image D first.
- During the writing task, coach students to complete one line at a time instead of drafting a paragraph.
- Keep the speaking task short and scaffolded by letting students use the saved card as support.

## Professional Context Notes
Fashion students regularly need to notice and name small visible details in critique, sketch notes, technical conversation, and early portfolio language. This week supports that observation habit by linking detail vocabulary to practical function and visual effect.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Some details can be both functional and decorative in fashion practice. Make room for that idea in class examples so students do not assume each feature fits only one category. Also clarify that tie in this week means a fastening detail, not a necktie accessory.

## Simplify This Week
- Use only four gallery details during the first pass: zipper, embroidery, patch pockets, and ruffle.
- Model the role language with sentence starters already visible.
- Accept short noun phrases in early practice before asking for full sentences in the writing card.

## Extend This Week
- Ask students to add one extra effect sentence with adds or makes.
- Invite students to compare two details from different gallery images.
- Ask stronger students to explain why one detail is both functional and decorative.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The asynchronous flow works well if the stable image keys remain visible and the app saves exact selections, matches, fill blanks, and structured lines. For teacher review, show the selected image key, the six required writing lines, the optional challenge line, the checklist, common errors, and feedback prompts. The speaking task should remain teacher-observed only with no recording or upload step.
