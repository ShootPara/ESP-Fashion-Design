# Week 11: Design Details

## Overview
Week 11 shifts from full sketch description to close observation of garment details. Students learn to notice and name visible details, say where they are placed, and explain whether they are functional, decorative, or both. The week ends with one scaffolded detail-caption card and one short teacher-supported speaking explanation.

## Learning Objectives
- Identify common garment design details such as closures, pockets, decorative features, and visible construction details.
- Use short sentence frames to say where a detail is and what it does.
- Distinguish between functional and decorative roles in simple English.
- Read and listen to short detail captions tied to stable image keys.
- Produce one structured design-detail caption card with six required short lines.

## Professional Fashion-Design Context
Students use the kind of English they need for early critique, sketch annotation, sewing and patternmaking observation, and short portfolio-caption writing. The focus on details supports later design explanation, feedback, and mini design-board work.

## Language Objectives
- Name details such as zipper, patch pocket, embroidery, and ruffle.
- Use location phrases such as on the front, near the neckline, and at the side.
- Use There is and There are for visible details.
- Use frames such as It is functional because ..., It is decorative because ..., It adds ..., and It makes ....

## Materials Needed
- Week 11 opener board with one full look and three close-up detail crops.
- Week 11 keyed vocabulary board A to J.
- Week 11 scavenger garment with point labels A to H.
- Week 11 reusable detail gallery image A to image D.
- Week 11 listening clip aligned to the reusable gallery.
- Optional projected or printed copies of the reusable gallery for the writing and speaking tasks.

## Suggested Sequence
1. Start with the opener so students move from one full look to close-up detail noticing.
2. Teach the core vocabulary through the keyed close-up board.
3. Use the scavenger garment to connect visible points to detail names and simple role language.
4. Model short captions through reading before the listening task.
5. Use the listening task with the same stable gallery to reinforce the same details.
6. Consolidate the language with short phrase-practice fill blanks.
7. Move into the main structured writing card with one chosen gallery image.
8. End the online sequence with two narrow correction cards.
9. Use the teacher session for pronunciation support and one short spoken explanation.

## Timing Estimate
The student-facing weekly flow is designed for about 141 minutes total, including a 50-minute teacher-observed speaking session.

## Differentiation Options
- Keep Portuguese instructions visible while students produce English answers.
- Pre-teach only four core detail words first for learners who need more support.
- Let stronger students use the optional challenge line in the writing card.
- During the speaking task, allow students to read from the saved caption card before speaking more freely.

## Extension Options
- Ask students to compare two details from the gallery using simple contrast language.
- Ask students to add one extra sentence with It makes the garment look ....
- Reuse the gallery for a short warm-up in Week 12 or Week 13.

## Notes for Future App Implementation
The app should keep stable visible keys on the screen whenever a task depends on them. The writing card should save the chosen image key plus each required line separately. The listening task should allow replay and reveal the script only after the first completed attempt. The teacher review flow should show the selected image key, the saved structured lines, a short checklist, common error prompts, and a return-for-revision option for the writing task only.
