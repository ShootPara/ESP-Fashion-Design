# Teacher Notes: Week 3 - Garments and Categories

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may confuse similar garment categories, especially `dress` and `skirt`, `jacket` and `blazer`, or `shirt` and `blouse`. They may also overuse singular frames with plural garments, producing sentences like `This are jeans` or `It has pockets` when the garment is plural. Another frequent issue is dropping articles in singular descriptions such as `This is dress` or writing too little in the Garment ID Card to show category, features, and use clearly.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the week highly visual and sequenced from recognition to production. Let students identify and sort garments before asking them to describe anything. During the Garment ID Card task, display the required sentence frames where everyone can see them and encourage students to complete one line at a time instead of trying to write freely. In the teacher speaking session, allow students to hold their card and use it as a speaking guide.

## Professional Context Notes
Keep examples connected to real Fashion Design study rather than general shopping language. Students should use garment words to talk about what they sketch, classify, present, and discuss in class. The week is not about fashion opinions only; it is about building a basic technical vocabulary for visible clothing categories, simple features, and use or occasion.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Some learners may already know international fashion words such as `look`, `blazer`, or `cardigan`, but they may not use them accurately in English sentences. Treat those words as useful bridges, while still modeling clear beginner grammar. It is also helpful to note that `pants` is the main production target for this course, while `trousers` is included mainly as a recognition variant students may see in other English materials.

## Simplify This Week
Reduce the Garment ID Card to exactly five required lines:
- This is a ___ / These are ___.
- It is a ___ / They are ___.
- It has ___ / They have ___.
- It is for ___ / They are for ___.
- I like it because ___ / I like them because ___.

For reading and listening, keep answer choices visible during the first pass. In the speaking task, accept 3 to 4 short spoken sentences with teacher prompting if needed.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to add the optional challenge line comparing one garment to another, or to add one extra feature phrase such as `It has short sleeves and buttons`. In the teacher session, invite a short follow-up question about weather, occasion, or whether the garment is casual or formal.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, vocabulary matching, reading, listening, phrase practice, and correction task can all be app-checked with immediate feedback. The Garment ID Card should remain teacher-visible because it is the week's main structured writing artifact and may need revision support. The speaking checkpoint should remain teacher-observed and non-recorded, with the app serving only to display prompts, sentence frames, garment images, and completion notes.
