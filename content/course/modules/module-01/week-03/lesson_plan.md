# Week 3: Garments and Categories

## Overview
Week 3 shifts the focus from studio tools to the garments Fashion Design students actually draw, organize, sew, describe, and later critique. The week stays highly visual and A2-first. Students begin with a fast visual opener, learn a core garment set through picture vocabulary and category sorting, read and listen to short garment descriptions, practice reliable sentence frames, build a scaffolded Garment ID Card, review one correction target, and then present one garment in a teacher-led speaking session.

The instructional sequence keeps the same recognizable rhythm as Weeks 1 and 2: recognition before production, structured support before teacher-reviewed writing, and written support before speaking. The Garment ID Card is the week’s main artifact, but it remains controlled and frame-based rather than paragraph-based.

## Learning Objectives
- Recognize and name common garments in English.
- Sort garments into simple categories such as tops, bottoms, one-piece garments, outerwear, and outfit or look.
- Understand short A2-level written and spoken garment descriptions.
- Use sentence frames such as `This is a ___`, `These are ___`, `It has ___`, `They have ___`, and `It is for ___`.
- Build a short Garment ID Card from sentence frames instead of writing an open paragraph from scratch.
- Revise one sentence focused on articles, plural garment forms, or missing noun-phrase elements.
- Say 4-6 short garment-description sentences during the teacher session.

## Professional Fashion-Design Context
Garment vocabulary is central to Fashion Design study. Students need this language to talk about sketches, references, silhouettes, wardrobe categories, classroom examples, styling decisions, and later design critique. Week 3 begins that process with practical, visible, reusable language rather than abstract fashion commentary.

The tasks are designed for adult learners in a fashion-studies context. Even at A2 level, students are naming garments, explaining simple features, and using clear category language that will matter again in later weeks about garment parts, textile choices, and design presentation.

## Language Objectives
- Name garments such as `T-shirt`, `shirt`, `blouse`, `sweater`, `hoodie`, `skirt`, `pants`, `shorts`, `jeans`, `dress`, `jumpsuit`, `jacket`, `coat`, and `blazer`.
- Use category language such as `top`, `bottom`, `outerwear`, `garment`, `outfit`, and `look`.
- Describe garments with short adjectives such as `casual`, `formal`, and `comfortable`.
- Name simple garment features such as `collar`, `buttons`, `zipper`, `pockets`, `short sleeves`, and `long sleeves`.
- Use singular and plural garment frames more accurately, especially with `pants`, `jeans`, and `shorts`.
- Connect garment description to use or occasion with phrases such as `for class`, `for work`, `for hot weather`, and `for cold weather`.

## Materials Needed
- One garment overview image board for the opener.
- Two to three garment vocabulary boards grouped by category.
- One reading board or image set for the three garment descriptions.
- One short garment-description audio clip or teacher read-aloud fallback.
- Sentence frames for singular and plural garment description.
- Student writing space for the Week 3 Garment ID Card.
- Optional garment image cards for teacher-led category sorting and live speaking support.

## Suggested Sequence
1. `m01w03-a01` Welcome Back: students identify common garments from a visual board.
2. `m01w03-a02` Garment Vocabulary: students match garment images to English names and sort them by category.
3. `m01w03-a03` Read: students match three short garment descriptions to the correct images and answer detail questions.
4. `m01w03-a04` Listen: students hear four short garment descriptions and choose the correct garment, feature, or category.
5. `m01w03-a05` Phrase Practice: students complete and correct controlled garment-description frames.
6. `m01w03-a06` Create a Garment ID Card: students build a short structured garment description from sentence frames.
7. `m01w03-a07` Review and Improve: students correct one common Week 3 sentence problem.
8. `m01w03-a08` Teacher Session: students pronounce garment words, sort live image cards, and present one garment.

## Differentiation Options
- Keep garment boards visible during vocabulary, reading, listening, and writing.
- Let learners who need more support choose from reduced word banks before completing sentence frames.
- Allow students to select either a singular garment or a plural garment for the Garment ID Card based on confidence.
- Provide one completed model frame beside the writing task for learners who need stronger support.
- Allow pair rehearsal with notes before the teacher-observed speaking task.

## Extension Options
- Ask stronger learners to add one optional challenge sentence explaining why a garment is useful, modern, or appropriate for a situation.
- Invite students to compare two garments briefly with a sentence such as `The blazer is formal, but the hoodie is casual`.
- During the teacher session, ask confident learners one follow-up question about use, weather, or category.

## Notes for Future App Implementation
Week 3 maps cleanly to a visual-first mobile flow. The app-facing design should preserve:

- one reusable garment overview board for the opener
- category-based image-word matching boards saved as the main checked output
- a reading board with short texts and image matching rather than long reading passages
- audio replay for the listening task with transcript reveal only after an attempt
- sentence-frame writing fields for the Garment ID Card instead of one large blank text box
- a teacher-visible structured writing submission with revision support
- a teacher-observed speaking checkpoint that tracks completion without recording audio

The garment set introduced this week should remain reusable in later weeks about garment parts, silhouettes, fabrics, styling, and simple critique.
