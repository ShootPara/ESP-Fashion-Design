# Teacher Notes: Week 13 - Giving Simple Design Feedback

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may know design vocabulary from earlier weeks but still give vague feedback such as `good` or `nice` without naming a real feature. Some learners may confuse a positive comment with a suggestion, or write `because` without a full reason clause. Students may also use `can` instead of `could` for polite critique, mix details from more than one gallery image, or turn the writing card into a short paragraph instead of six separate lines.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the reusable `image A` to `image D` gallery visible throughout the week. Students should be able to point to the same image during reading, listening, writing, and speaking tasks. Move steadily from recognition to production: opener, phrase bank, sentence classification, reading, listening, fill blanks, structured writing, correction, then speaking. During the writing task, remind students to complete one line at a time instead of drafting freely.

## Professional Context Notes
Fashion students regularly need to respond to sketches, captions, and early design ideas in critique sessions. This week gives them a beginner-friendly way to do that in English. The goal is not advanced criticism. The goal is useful studio language that can name one strength, explain one reason, offer one respectful suggestion, and state one revision step before the final mini design board.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Explain that feedback in design class should be useful and respectful, not only positive or only negative. `Suggestion` should be taught as polite advice, not a command. `Revision` means a change made after feedback, not just a general review. Also note that borrowed words such as `feedback` may feel familiar to learners, but the full English critique phrases still need practice.

## Simplify This Week
- Keep the main feedback structure visible at all times: positive comment, reason, suggestion, revision plan.
- Accept short design-focused answers before asking for complete longer lines in the writing card.
- Use only the clearest gallery image first for students who need more support.
- Keep the writing card at exactly six required lines:
  - `I like the ___.`
  - `It works well because ___.`
  - `The strongest part is ___.`
  - `You could ___.`
  - `I will revise ___.`
  - `This will make the design more ___.`
- Use the optional challenge line only if the student is ready:
  - `Maybe you could ___ because ___.`
  The optional challenge line is not required for completion. Save and show it separately only if the student completes it.

## Extend This Week
- Ask stronger learners to add one extra effect sentence with `This creates ...` or `This makes the look ...`.
- Invite stronger learners to compare two gallery images and explain which one receives stronger feedback.
- In the teacher session, ask one follow-up question such as `Why does that detail work well?` or `What will you revise first?`

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, phrase bank, sentence-classification task, reading task, listening task, fill-blank task, and correction task can all be app-checked with immediate feedback if the answer data stays explicit. The Design Feedback Note + Revision Plan Card should remain teacher-visible because it is the main written artifact, uses six required lines plus one optional challenge line, and benefits from one revision cycle. The optional line should appear separately in teacher review only if the student completed it, and the required task should not be marked incomplete when that optional line is blank. The speaking checkpoint should remain teacher-observed and non-recorded, with the app serving only to display the selected gallery image from `m01w13-a07`, the saved card, the speaking frames, and completion notes.
