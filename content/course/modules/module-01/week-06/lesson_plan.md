# Week 6: Color, Shape, and Line

## Overview
Week 6 introduces the English students need to describe what a look looks like visually. After Weeks 3 to 5, students can already name garments, garment parts, and figure features. This week adds color, palette, shape, line, silhouette, and simple visual-impression language so students can move from naming an item to describing the whole look. The week stays visual, concrete, and A2-first.

The main written artifact is not a paragraph. Students complete a scaffolded Color + Shape Look Description with 6 required sentence-frame lines and 1 optional challenge sentence. This keeps the task aligned with early Module 1 writing guardrails while still preparing students for later portfolio-style captions and design-description work.

## Learning Objectives
- Recognize and name basic colors and color-family words in English.
- Identify simple palette types such as warm, cool, neutral, bright, soft, and monochrome.
- Match shape, line, and silhouette vocabulary to clear visual examples.
- Read short visual look descriptions and connect them to the correct keyed images.
- Listen to short spoken look descriptions and identify the correct image or detail.
- Use simple frames such as `The color is ...`, `The palette is ...`, `The silhouette is ...`, `The lines are ...`, and `This look is ...`.
- Practice adjective order in phrases such as `a long black dress` and `a soft pink blouse`.
- Complete a scaffolded look-description card with 6 required lines and 1 optional challenge line.
- Describe one look orally in 4 to 6 short sentences during the teacher session.

## Professional Fashion-Design Context
Week 6 connects English to beginner drawing classes, creativity labs, look analysis, and early design description. Fashion Design students need this language when they talk about sketches, silhouettes, color palettes, and visual impressions before they explain deeper concept or technical details.

The week also prepares students for later modules on textiles, moodboards, styling, collections, portfolio captions, and design feedback. The focus should stay on respectful visual observation and design language, not on personal judgment about bodies or appearance.

## Language Objectives
- Name basic colors such as `black`, `white`, `red`, `blue`, `green`, `yellow`, `pink`, `purple`, `orange`, `brown`, and `gray`.
- Use palette words such as `warm`, `cool`, `neutral`, `bright`, `soft`, `monochrome`, and `contrast`.
- Use line and shape language such as `straight line`, `curved line`, `vertical line`, `horizontal line`, `diagonal line`, `round`, `square`, and `triangular`.
- Use silhouette and garment-shape words such as `loose`, `fitted`, `straight`, `flared`, `oversized`, and `cropped`.
- Use description frames such as `The palette is warm`, `The silhouette is long and straight`, `The lines are curved`, and `I like the look because it is simple`.
- Improve adjective order in simple garment phrases such as `a long black dress` and `wide blue pants`.

## Materials Needed
- One Week 6 opener board with 3 to 4 clearly different looks.
- One color swatch and palette board with visible keys.
- One shape, line, and silhouette board with visible keys.
- One three-look reading gallery labeled `image A`, `image B`, and `image C`.
- One reusable four-look gallery labeled `image A`, `image B`, `image C`, and `image D`.
- One short listening audio clip or teacher read-aloud fallback for the listening task.
- Visible sentence frames for the Week 6 language focus.
- Student writing space for the Color + Shape Look Description.

## Suggested Sequence
1. `m01w06-a01` Welcome Back: students look at a multi-look board and choose what they notice first.
2. `m01w06-a02` Color Vocabulary and Palettes: students complete the main color and palette matching set, then use a short Portuguese bridge and one support item.
3. `m01w06-a03` Shape, Line, and Silhouette: students complete the main shape-line-silhouette matching set and use one support item after the core matching.
4. `m01w06-a04` Read: students match three short visual descriptions to `image A`, `image B`, or `image C` and answer detail questions.
5. `m01w06-a05` Listen: students hear four short look descriptions and choose the matching image or key detail.
6. `m01w06-a06` Phrase Practice: students complete adjective-order and look-description frames with fill-blank support.
7. `m01w06-a07` Color + Shape Look Description: students choose one look and complete a teacher-reviewed structured writing card.
8. `m01w06-a08` Review and Improve: students correct one common Week 6 sentence problem.
9. `m01w06-a09` Teacher Session: students practice pronunciation and describe one look in short supported sentences.

## Differentiation Options
- Keep the color board, shape board, and image galleries visible during vocabulary, reading, listening, writing, and speaking.
- Reduce the number of required palette terms for learners who need more support.
- Allow students to choose from two simpler looks before moving to a more complex look for the writing task.
- Provide a reduced word bank for the writing card if students need more support with silhouette or palette language.
- Let students point to the image while speaking in the teacher session.

## Extension Options
- Ask stronger learners to add the optional challenge sentence comparing two visual elements in the same look.
- Invite stronger learners to add one reason sentence about why the palette or silhouette works.
- In the teacher session, ask one follow-up question about contrast, line direction, or visual impression.

## Notes for Future App Implementation
Week 6 should remain strongly image-first and tightly aligned across tasks in a future app. The app-facing flow should preserve:

- one opener board for quick preview and review
- one main color-and-palette matching set as the primary saved vocabulary output
- one main shape-line-silhouette matching set as the primary saved visual-vocabulary output
- one reading gallery with explicit visible keys `image A`, `image B`, and `image C`
- one listening task whose audio script matches the four-look gallery and answer key exactly
- one fill-blank phrase-practice task where the primary saved output stays phrase completion
- one teacher-reviewed structured writing card with separate sentence-frame fields instead of a paragraph box
- one teacher-observed speaking checkpoint that shows the gallery and frames but does not record audio

The Week 6 four-look gallery should stay reusable for listening, writing, speaking, and later visual-description work in future modules.
