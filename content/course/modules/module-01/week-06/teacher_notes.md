# Teacher Notes: Week 6 - Color, Shape, and Line

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may confuse color words that are close in meaning or overuse only basic colors without adding palette or visual-impression language. They may also mix `shape`, `line`, and `silhouette` as if they mean the same thing. Another common difficulty is adjective order, especially in phrases like `a black long dress`, and agreement problems such as `The pants is blue` or `The color palette are warm`.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the Week 6 boards and galleries visible throughout the week. Students should be able to point to color, line, and silhouette examples while they answer. Move steadily from recognition to production: opener, color matching, shape-line matching, reading, listening, phrase practice, structured writing, correction, then speaking. During the writing task, encourage students to complete one sentence frame at a time instead of trying to produce a paragraph.

## Professional Context Notes
Keep the week connected to fashion drawing, beginner visual analysis, and look description. Students are not doing abstract art criticism. They are learning how to say what they see in a look: color palette, garment shapes, visible lines, silhouette, and overall impression. This language is useful for later sketch explanation, moodboards, styling comments, and short portfolio captions.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Many learners may already know fashion words such as `silhouette`, `oversized`, or `cropped` from social media or design classes, but they may not know how to place them in a full English sentence. Keep the focus on accurate simple usage, not on sounding advanced. Also remind students that visual-impression words such as `modern`, `classic`, `casual`, and `playful` are design-description words, not personal judgments.

## Simplify This Week
Keep the writing card at exactly six required lines:
- This is a look / sketch / fashion figure.
- The color palette is ___.
- The figure is wearing ___.
- The silhouette is ___.
- The lines are ___.
- The look is ___.

Accept one optional challenge sentence only if the student is ready: `I like the ___ because ___.`. In the speaking task, accept 3 to 4 short spoken sentences with strong teacher prompting if needed.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to use the optional challenge sentence comparing two elements, for example `The top is fitted, but the pants are loose.` During the teacher session, invite one follow-up sentence explaining why the student likes the palette, silhouette, or contrast.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, color matching, shape-line-silhouette matching, reading task, listening task, phrase-practice task, and correction task can all be app-checked with immediate feedback. The Color + Shape Look Description should remain teacher-visible because it is the main written artifact, uses 6 required lines plus 1 optional challenge sentence, and benefits from a small revision cycle. The speaking checkpoint should remain teacher-observed and non-recorded, with the app serving only to display the gallery, sentence frames, and completion notes.
