# Teacher Notes: Week 10 - Describing a Sketch

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may know the vocabulary in isolation but struggle to combine it into one clear description. Common problems include using `using` instead of `is wearing`, writing noun lists instead of complete sentences, mixing up `color palette` and `silhouette`, and transferring Portuguese adjective order into English phrases such as `jacket denim short` instead of `short denim jacket`. Some learners may also forget plural details such as `buttons` and `pockets`, or use `because` without a full reason clause.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the reading gallery and the listening gallery visible throughout the week. Students should be able to point to the sketch while they answer. Move steadily from recognition to production: opener, vocabulary review, reading, listening, ordering, phrase practice, structured writing, correction, then speaking. During the writing task, remind students to complete one line at a time instead of trying to produce a paragraph from memory.

## Professional Context Notes
Keep the week connected to sketch presentation, beginner portfolio caption language, and short classroom explanation of a design look. Students are not doing advanced critique yet. They are learning how to explain what the figure is wearing, what colors and silhouette are visible, what details stand out, what fabric could fit the look, and what the overall design impression is.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Many learners may already have strong Portuguese vocabulary for sketch, silhouette, palette, and fabric categories, but the English phrases may still feel unstable. Keep the target language practical and repeated. Also explain that Fashion Design sketch descriptions often move from general impression to specific details, which is why the organization task matters this week.

## Simplify This Week
Keep the writing card at exactly six required lines:
- `This sketch shows a figure in ___.`
- `The figure is wearing ___.`
- `The garment has ___.`
- `The color palette is ___.`
- `The silhouette is ___.`
- `I imagine this garment in ___.`

Use the shared `image A` to `image C` gallery as a visible reference and have the student save one selected sketch key before writing. Accept one optional challenge line only if the student is ready: `I chose this sketch because ___.` Mark the writing card by checking the saved sketch reference, the six required lines, visible sketch alignment, complete sentence use, one clear garment detail, one clear color-palette line, one clear silhouette line, and one simple fabric idea. In the speaking task, accept 5 short spoken sentences with strong teacher prompting if needed.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to add the optional challenge line with one design reason. During the teacher session, invite one follow-up sentence about inspiration, comfort, or function, such as `I chose this fabric because it looks comfortable.` or `The design feels playful and modern.`

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, vocabulary review, reading task, listening task, ordering task, phrase-practice task, and correction task can all be app-checked with immediate feedback. The Fashion Sketch Description Card should remain teacher-visible because it is the main written artifact, uses 6 required lines plus 1 optional challenge sentence, and benefits from a small revision cycle. The speaking checkpoint should remain teacher-observed and non-recorded, with the app serving only to display the shared gallery, sentence frames, and completion notes.
