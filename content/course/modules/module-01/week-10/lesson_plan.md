# Week 10: Describing a Sketch

## Overview
Week 10 is the first major integration week in Module 1. Students have already built vocabulary for garments, garment parts, figure and pose, color and silhouette, fabric and texture, sewing actions, and beginner patternmaking. This week they combine those pieces into one clear sketch description.

The main written artifact stays A2-first. Students do not write a paragraph and do not work toward a base word count. Instead, they complete a scaffolded Fashion Sketch Description Card with 6 required sentence-frame lines and 1 optional challenge sentence. This keeps the writing concrete, teacher-reviewable, and aligned with early Module 1 guardrails while still feeling like a milestone task.

## Learning Objectives
- Recognize and review integrated sketch-description vocabulary from earlier Module 1 weeks.
- Match English sketch-description words to Portuguese support and sort them into clear categories.
- Read three short sketch descriptions and match them to the correct keyed images.
- Listen to four numbered sketch descriptions and identify the correct image and visible details.
- Organize a simple sketch description from opening sentence to final design comment.
- Complete phrase frames such as `The figure is wearing ...`, `The garment has ...`, `The color palette is ...`, and `I chose ... because ...`.
- Complete a scaffolded Fashion Sketch Description Card with 6 required lines and 1 optional challenge line.
- Present one sketch orally in 5 to 6 short supported sentences during the teacher session.

## Professional Fashion-Design Context
Week 10 connects English to the kind of basic visual explanation Fashion Design students need when they talk about a croquis, explain a class sketch, or prepare a simple portfolio-style caption. The focus is not advanced critique. Students are learning how to say what the figure is doing, what the outfit looks like, what details are visible, what fabric could fit the design, and what the overall look communicates.

This week also prepares students for later work on design details, feedback, inspiration, and mini design boards. The description order matters professionally: identify the sketch, describe the outfit, mention color and silhouette, add details and fabric, then close with a short design comment.

## Language Objectives
- Name figure and sketch words such as `sketch`, `croquis`, `figure`, `pose`, `front view`, and `side view`.
- Name garment words such as `dress`, `jacket`, `top`, `pants`, `skirt`, and `outfit`.
- Name visible details such as `sleeve`, `neckline`, `pocket`, `zipper`, `hem`, and `buttons`.
- Use design-description words such as `color palette`, `silhouette`, `neutral`, `bright`, `soft`, `straight`, `loose`, `fitted`, and `flared`.
- Use fabric and design-comment words such as `cotton`, `denim`, `lightweight`, `structured`, `flowy`, `main feature`, `overall look`, `comfortable`, `playful`, and `elegant`.
- Use short integrated frames such as `The figure is wearing ...`, `The garment has ...`, `The color palette is ...`, `The silhouette is ...`, `I imagine this garment in ...`, and `The overall look is ...`.

## Materials Needed
- One Week 10 opener sketch board with visible keys and one clearly readable fashion look.
- One reusable sketch gallery labeled `image A`, `image B`, and `image C` for reading, writing, and speaking.
- One listening sketch gallery labeled `image A`, `image B`, `image C`, and `image D`.
- One short Week 10 listening audio clip or teacher read-aloud fallback using the same `image A` to `image D` listening gallery.
- Visible sentence frames for Week 10 sketch-description language.
- Student writing space for the Fashion Sketch Description Card.
- Optional classroom support: printed gallery cards, a projector, color swatches, or teacher-selected sketch references.

## Suggested Sequence
1. `m01w10-a01` Welcome Back: students look at one keyed sketch board and answer five short review questions about garment, detail, palette, silhouette, and possible fabric.
2. `m01w10-a02` Sketch Description Vocabulary Review: students complete one main English-Portuguese matching and category-sorting set for integrated Week 10 vocabulary.
3. `m01w10-a03` Read: students use the `image A` to `image C` gallery, read three short descriptions, and answer image-match and detail questions.
4. `m01w10-a04` Listen: students use the `image A` to `image D` gallery, listen to one numbered audio clip, and answer image and detail questions that match the script exactly.
5. `m01w10-a05` Organize the Description: students put six description sentences in logical order and complete two short support checks.
6. `m01w10-a06` Phrase Practice: students complete six fill-blank phrase frames for sketch, garment, color, silhouette, fabric, and design comment language.
7. `m01w10-a07` Fashion Sketch Description Card: students choose one sketch from the shared reading gallery and complete a teacher-reviewed structured writing card.
8. `m01w10-a08` Review and Improve: students correct two short Week 10 sketch-description sentences and save the improved versions.
9. `m01w10-a09` Teacher Session: students practice pronunciation and present one sketch in 5 to 6 short supported sentences.

## Differentiation Options
- Keep the reading gallery and the listening gallery visible while students answer.
- Reduce the active target language to `sketch`, `figure`, `jacket`, `dress`, `color palette`, `silhouette`, `cotton`, and `overall look` for learners who need more support.
- Let students choose the simplest reading-gallery sketch for the writing card, such as the soft dress or denim jacket look.
- Provide a reduced word bank for the writing card if students need support with design-comment or fabric phrases.
- Let students point to visible sketch details while speaking in the teacher session.

## Extension Options
- Ask stronger learners to add the optional challenge sentence with one simple reason, such as `I chose denim because it looks strong and practical.`
- Invite stronger learners to add one extra line about inspiration or one additional visible detail after the main speaking frame.
- In the teacher session, ask one short follow-up question such as `Why did you choose this fabric?` or `What is the main feature?`

## Notes for Future App Implementation
Week 10 should preserve one stable interaction flow from recognition to production:

- one opener sketch board for a fast integration review
- one main vocabulary matching and sorting set as the primary saved vocabulary output
- one reading task with a fixed visible-key strategy `image A` to `image C`
- one listening task whose audio script matches the `image A` to `image D` listening gallery and answer key exactly
- one sentence-order task where the main saved output stays ordering
- one fill-blank phrase-practice task where the primary saved output stays sentence completion
- one teacher-reviewed structured writing card with separate sentence-frame fields instead of a paragraph box
- one teacher-observed speaking checkpoint that shows the selected sketch and speaking frames but does not record audio

The reading gallery should stay consistent across reading, writing, and speaking so students do not have to relearn a new set of images when they move into the main written and spoken artifacts.
