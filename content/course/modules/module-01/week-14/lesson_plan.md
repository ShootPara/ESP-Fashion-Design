# Week 14: Mini Design Board

## Overview
Week 14 is the final integration week for Module 1. Students bring together garment vocabulary, parts, silhouette, palette, fabric, detail, inspiration, and revision language in one small but meaningful artifact: a Mini Design Board in English.

The week keeps the Module 1 A2 guardrails. Students do not write a paragraph and do not work toward a word-count target. Instead, they build the board in small saved pieces: one selected image key, eight required labels, one title, five short required lines, one optional challenge line, one revision target, and one final teacher-reviewed submission. This makes the final week app-ready, concrete, and manageable for beginner learners.

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the key sections of a mini design board and explain what each section does.
- Review cumulative Module 1 vocabulary for garments, parts, fabric, details, inspiration, and revision.
- Read a model mini design board and answer section-based comprehension questions.
- Listen to a short model mini-board presentation and identify exact title, detail, fabric, inspiration, and sequence information.
- Choose one gallery image and match eight labels to visible board slots.
- Build a draft mini design board with one title, five required lines, and one optional challenge line.
- Revise one line with a specific checklist and correction target.
- Submit one final structured mini design board artifact.
- Give one short teacher-observed presentation with sentence-frame support.

## Professional Fashion-Design Context
Fashion students often need to show an image, add short labels, describe a look, explain a fabric or detail choice, and say where the idea comes from. Week 14 gives students a first English version of that process. The mini design board is not a full portfolio page, but it is a useful bridge to future moodboards, design captions, and studio presentation tasks.

This week also makes earlier module language more functional. Students move from isolated weekly outputs to one integrated artifact that can support classroom sharing, critique, and future portfolio-style work.

## Language Objectives
- Use presentation frames such as `This board shows ...` and `The title is ...`.
- Use description frames such as `The figure is wearing ...`, `The garment has ...`, and `The silhouette is ...`.
- Use explanation frames such as `I imagine this garment in ...`, `The main detail is ...`, and `This design is inspired by ...`.
- Use reason language with `because`.
- Use final-comment and revision language such as `The overall look is ...` and `I revised ... to make it clearer.`

## Materials Needed
- One Week 14 model mini design board with visible section labels and short readable text.
- One Week 14 cumulative review board with visible keys and category clues.
- One reusable Week 14 four-panel gallery labeled only `image A`, `image B`, `image C`, and `image D`.
- One Week 14 listening clip aligned exactly to the model board.
- Visible sentence frames for description, fabric/detail, inspiration, rationale, and final comment.
- Student writing space for draft and final structured board fields.
- Optional classroom support: projected gallery, printed image board, or teacher-selected display copies.

## Suggested Sequence
1. `m01w14-a01` Welcome Back: students study one finished model board and answer quick questions about the required sections.
2. `m01w14-a02` Mini Design Board Vocabulary Review: students complete one cumulative matching task with visible image keys and category labels.
3. `m01w14-a03` Read: students read the model mini design board and answer section-based main idea and detail questions.
4. `m01w14-a04` Listen: students use the same model board, listen to one short presentation, and answer aligned comprehension items.
5. `m01w14-a05` Board Builder: students choose one gallery image from `image A` to `image D` and match eight required labels.
6. `m01w14-a06` Build the Text Sections: students use the chosen image and saved labels to complete one title, five required lines, and one optional challenge line.
7. `m01w14-a07` Review and Improve: students complete a checklist, choose one revision target, and rewrite one line.
8. `m01w14-a08` Mini Design Board Submission: students review and submit the final structured artifact for teacher review.
9. `m01w14-a09` Teacher Session: students use the submitted board and speaking frames to give one short supported mini presentation.

## Differentiation Options
- Keep Portuguese instructions visible while students produce English labels and sentence frames.
- Keep the same `image A` to `image D` gallery visible across the board-builder, draft, submission, and speaking tasks.
- Offer a reduced word bank for students who need more support choosing garment, fabric, and inspiration labels.
- Let students choose the clearest gallery image first rather than the most complex one.
- During the speaking task, allow students to read from the submitted board before speaking more freely.

## Extension Options
- Ask stronger learners to complete the optional challenge line: `I chose ___ because it creates ___.`
- Ask stronger learners to add one extra spoken sentence comparing their board with the model board.
- Use the final boards as a bridge into later modules on moodboards, collection ideas, or portfolio captions.

## Notes for Future App Implementation
Week 14 should preserve one clear final-week interaction flow:

- one model mini board for opener, reading, and listening reuse
- one cumulative matching review where matching remains the primary saved output
- one reusable four-panel gallery labeled only `image A` to `image D`
- one board-builder activity where the selected image key is saved once and reused in later tasks
- one structured draft with separate saved fields for title, five required lines, and one optional challenge line
- one revision task where the checklist is support and the revised line is the main saved output
- one teacher-reviewed final submission with separate visible fields instead of a paragraph box
- one teacher-observed speaking task that displays the submitted board as read-only support and does not record audio

The final board should stay export-ready in future phases because its image key, labels, and text sections are already separated into structured fields.
