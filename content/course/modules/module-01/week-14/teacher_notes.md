# Teacher Notes: Week 14 - Mini Design Board

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may know the weekly vocabulary but still struggle to combine it into one coherent final artifact. Some learners may choose labels that do not match the fixed sample panel `image B`, mix details from different gallery images, or skip one section of the board. Others may turn the writing task into a paragraph instead of following the structured lines. Typical language problems include missing `is` in `The figure is wearing ...`, weak `because` clauses, confusion between fabric and detail language, and uncertainty with pronunciation of `silhouette`, `palette`, `neckline`, and `inspiration`.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the model board and the reusable `image A` to `image D` gallery visible during the key tasks. Move steadily from recognition to production: opener, review, reading, listening, label matching, structured draft, checklist revision, final submission, and teacher-supported presentation. During the writing tasks, remind students that the title plus five required lines are the complete minimum product. Do not expand the task into open paragraph writing.

## Professional Context Notes
The mini design board is a realistic beginner studio task. Fashion students often need to label a look, describe the main garment, explain a fabric or detail choice, and give a short inspiration reason. Week 14 packages those functions into one English artifact that can support later moodboards, collection concepts, portfolio captions, and short presentations.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Explain that short board text is normal in design communication. A good board is not always long. The goal is clarity, useful labels, and a few meaningful explanation lines. Also remind students that English design words such as `silhouette`, `palette`, `detail`, and `label` may look familiar or borrowed, but they still need correct use in English sentence frames.

## Simplify This Week
- Keep the final board artifact visible as a structured set, not as a paragraph:
  - fixed saved image key `image B`
  - saved label set from Activity 5
  - title
  - `This board shows ...`
  - `The figure is wearing ...`
  - `I imagine this garment in ...`
  - `This design is inspired by ... because ...`
  - `The overall look is ...`
  - one final revision comment line such as `I revised ... to make it clearer.`
- Keep the required structured writing at exactly six saved required fields from Activity 6:
  - title
  - `This board shows ...`
  - `The figure is wearing ...`
  - `I imagine this garment in ...`
  - `This design is inspired by ... because ...`
  - `The overall look is ...`
- Use the optional challenge line only if the student is ready:
  - `The main detail is ___ because ___.`
  The optional challenge line is not required for completion and should be saved separately only if the student completes it.
- In Activity 8, review the structured final board artifact field by field:
  - fixed image key
  - label set
  - five required board lines plus title
  - final revision comment
- Do not turn the final submission into open paragraph writing.

## Extend This Week
- Ask stronger learners to make the optional challenge line more specific with `functional` or `decorative`.
- Ask stronger learners to add one extra spoken line about revision during the teacher session.
- Invite stronger learners to compare the model board and their own board using one sentence with `Both ...` or `My board is more ...`.

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
Activities 1 to 5 can be app-checked if the visible answer clues remain explicit. Activity 6 should stay self-checked only; it is a structured draft, not a teacher-marked artifact in this pass. Activity 7 should remain a student revision step with one selected target and one rewritten line, without adding teacher workload. Activity 8 should be the only meaningful teacher-reviewed writing task of the week and should show the fixed image key, the matching gallery image, the labels, each saved text field, a checklist, common errors, feedback prompts, and a return-for-revision option. Activity 9 should remain teacher-observed only, with the app serving to show the submitted board and speaking frames without recording, upload, or automated speech scoring.
