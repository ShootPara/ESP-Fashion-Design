# Teacher Notes: Week 9 - Introduction to Patternmaking English

## Common Learner Difficulties
Students may confuse `pattern` with `pattern piece`, or use a garment word when they need a piece name such as `sleeve pattern piece`. They may also mix up `waist` and `hip`, or confuse a body measurement with a pattern marking. Another common difficulty is leaving out articles in phrases such as `Mark the grainline` or dropping the fixed phrase `on the fold`. In writing, students may try to list vocabulary words instead of completing the sentence-frame lines.

## Classroom Management Suggestions
Keep the Week 9 keyed boards visible throughout the week. Students should be able to point to a marking or a piece while they answer. Move steadily from recognition to production: opener, pattern-piece matching, measurement-and-mark matching, reading, listening, phrase practice, structured writing, correction, then speaking. During the writing task, encourage students to complete one line at a time instead of trying to explain the whole pattern piece from memory.

## Professional Context Notes
Keep the week connected to beginner patternmaking, paper pattern use, measurement checking, and simple garment-construction preparation. Students are not doing advanced drafting explanation yet. They are learning how to say what a piece is called, what garment it belongs to, what marking appears on it, and what simple actions they take to prepare it.

## Cultural or Industry Notes
Many learners may already know strong Portuguese vocabulary for modelagem and garment parts, but the English equivalents may not feel stable yet. Keep the English target practical and high-frequency. Also explain that `block` is a basic starting pattern, but it should stay a light support word this week rather than the main production target.

## Simplify This Week
Keep the writing card at exactly six required lines:
- `This is a ___ pattern piece.`
- `This piece is for a ___.`
- `It has a ___ and a ___.`
- `First, ___.`
- `Then, ___.`
- `Finally, check ___.`

Use the shared `image A` to `image E` gallery as a visual reference, not as a scored choice during the writing task. Accept one optional challenge sentence only if the student is ready: `This piece is on the fold because ___.` Mark the writing card by checking the six required lines, the correct piece name, at least one accurate marking, one clear process action, and the final check line. In the speaking task, accept 5 short spoken sentences with strong teacher prompting if needed.

## Extend This Week
Ask stronger learners to add the optional challenge sentence with a clear reason. During the teacher session, invite one follow-up sentence about placement such as `The notch is at the side seam` or purpose such as `This piece helps make the front of the skirt.`

## Asynchronous or App-Based Adaptation
The opener, pattern-pieces matching, measurements-and-marks matching, reading task, listening task, phrase-practice task, and correction task can all be app-checked with immediate feedback. The Basic Pattern Piece Description Card should remain teacher-visible because it is the main written artifact, uses 6 required lines plus 1 optional challenge sentence, and benefits from a small revision cycle. The speaking checkpoint should remain teacher-observed and non-recorded, with the app serving only to display the shared pattern-piece gallery, sentence frames, and completion notes.
