# Week 9: Introduction to Patternmaking English

## Overview
Week 9 turns Week 8 process language into introductory patternmaking English. Students already know how to talk about measuring, marking, and checking in a simple sewing sequence. This week they apply that process language to pattern pieces, body and garment measurements, and visible pattern markings such as grainline, fold line, seam allowance, notch, and dart.

The main written artifact stays A2-first. Students do not write a paragraph and do not work toward a base word count. Instead, they complete a scaffolded Basic Pattern Piece Description Card with 6 required sentence-frame lines and 1 optional challenge sentence. This keeps the writing concrete, teacher-reviewable, and aligned with early Module 1 guardrails.

## Learning Objectives
- Recognize and name common patternmaking tools, pattern pieces, and markings in English.
- Match measurement and pattern-mark vocabulary to clear visual references.
- Read a short patternmaking procedure and answer sequence and detail questions.
- Listen to a short pattern-piece instruction sequence and identify the correct image key, measurement, marking, and final label.
- Use short sentence frames such as `This is a sleeve pattern piece` and `This piece is for a skirt`.
- Use imperative language such as `Measure the waist`, `Mark the grainline`, `Trace the pattern piece`, and `Check the measurement`.
- Complete a scaffolded Basic Pattern Piece Description Card with 6 required lines and 1 optional challenge line.
- Explain one pattern piece orally in 5 to 6 short supported sentences during the teacher session.

## Professional Fashion-Design Context
Week 9 connects English to beginner patternmaking, garment-construction preparation, and visual reading of pattern diagrams. Fashion Design students need this language when they identify a pattern piece, follow a demonstration, label a basic shape, or explain what the piece is for.

The week also prepares students for later work on sketch description, construction planning, prototyping, and technical explanation. The focus should stay concrete and visible: what the piece is called, what garment it helps make, what measurement or marking appears on it, and what action happens first, next, and finally.

## Language Objectives
- Name patternmaking basics such as `patternmaking`, `pattern`, `pattern piece`, `pattern paper`, `block`, and `template`.
- Name specific pieces such as `front bodice`, `back bodice`, `sleeve`, `skirt front`, `skirt back`, `waistband`, `collar`, and `pocket`.
- Name measurements such as `bust`, `waist`, `hip`, `shoulder`, `armhole`, `sleeve length`, and `garment length`.
- Name pattern markings such as `grainline`, `fold line`, `seam allowance`, `notch`, `dart`, `center front`, `center back`, and `side seam`.
- Use process verbs such as `measure`, `mark`, `trace`, `label`, `check`, and `adjust`.
- Use location phrases such as `on the fold`, `along the line`, `at the center front`, and `at the side seam`.

## Materials Needed
- One Week 9 patternmaking workspace opener board with visible keys.
- One pattern-pieces-and-tools board labeled with visible keys `A` to `J`.
- One measurements-and-marks board labeled with visible keys `A` to `J`.
- One reading sequence board labeled with visible keys `A` to `F`.
- One reusable pattern-piece gallery labeled `image A`, `image B`, `image C`, `image D`, and `image E`.
- One short listening audio clip or teacher read-aloud fallback for the listening task.
- Visible sentence frames for Week 9 patternmaking language.
- Student writing space for the Basic Pattern Piece Description Card.
- Optional classroom support: paper pattern examples, a measuring tape, a ruler, a French curve, and printed pattern-piece cards.

## Suggested Sequence
1. `m01w09-a01` Welcome Back: students bridge from Week 8 sewing process language into Week 9 patternmaking language with one compact opener board.
2. `m01w09-a02` Patternmaking Vocabulary: students complete the main pattern-pieces-and-tools matching set, then use a short Portuguese bridge and one support sentence after the core matching.
3. `m01w09-a03` Measurements and Pattern Marks: students complete the main measurement-and-mark matching set using one clear keyed board.
4. `m01w09-a04` Read: students read a short patternmaking procedure, use the keyed sequence board, and answer first-step, next-step, marking, and detail questions.
5. `m01w09-a05` Listen: students hear one short pattern-piece instruction sequence and answer explicit image-key, measurement, marking, and label questions using the shared `image A` to `image E` gallery.
6. `m01w09-a06` Phrase Practice: students complete fill-blank frames using patternmaking imperatives and short purpose phrases.
7. `m01w09-a07` Basic Pattern Piece Description Card: students choose one simple pattern piece from the shared gallery and complete a teacher-reviewed structured writing card.
8. `m01w09-a08` Review and Improve: students correct two short Week 9 patternmaking sentences and save the improved versions.
9. `m01w09-a09` Teacher Session: students practice pronunciation and explain one pattern piece in short supported sentences.

## Differentiation Options
- Keep the keyed boards and the reusable gallery visible during reading, listening, writing, and speaking.
- Reduce the active target language to `pattern piece`, `waist`, `grainline`, `measure`, `mark`, `trace`, `label`, and `check` for learners who need more support.
- Let students choose the simplest piece from the gallery for the writing card, such as `skirt front` or `pocket`.
- Provide a reduced word bank for the writing card if students need more support with markings or location phrases.
- Let students point to the visible pattern-piece image while speaking in the teacher session.

## Extension Options
- Ask stronger learners to add the optional challenge sentence with a clear reason such as why the grainline or notch matters.
- Invite stronger learners to add one extra location sentence in speaking such as `The notch is at the side seam.`
- In the teacher session, ask one short follow-up question about why a marking matters or what garment the piece belongs to.

## Notes for Future App Implementation
Week 9 should stay tightly aligned across image keys, pattern markings, and listening answers in a future app. The app-facing flow should preserve:

- one opener board for a fast bridge from sewing to patternmaking
- one main pattern-pieces-and-tools matching set as the primary saved vocabulary output
- one main measurements-and-marks matching set as a second primary visual-vocabulary output
- one reading task with a fixed visible-key strategy `A` to `F`
- one listening task whose audio script matches the shared pattern-piece gallery and answer key exactly
- one fill-blank phrase-practice task where the primary saved output stays sentence completion
- one teacher-reviewed structured writing card with separate sentence-frame fields instead of a paragraph box
- one teacher-observed speaking checkpoint that shows the pattern-piece gallery and frames but does not record audio

The Week 9 reusable pattern-piece gallery should stay consistent across listening, writing, and speaking, and should use the same visible image keys across those tasks.
