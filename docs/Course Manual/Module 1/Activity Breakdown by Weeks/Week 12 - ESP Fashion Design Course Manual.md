## Week 12 purpose

Week 12 moves from describing what students can see to explaining **where a design idea comes from**. Students have already described garments, parts, figures, color, shape, line, fabric, process, pattern pieces, sketches, and design details. Now they begin to connect those visible features to inspiration and intention.

This week is the bridge between pure description and design rationale. The student learns simple language for saying:

- what inspired the design
    
- what visual elements come from that inspiration
    
- why they chose a color, fabric, shape, or detail
    
- what mood or impression they want the look to communicate
    

The main output is a **Design Inspiration Note**: a short 90–130 word text explaining the inspiration for a simple look or sketch.

## Reading text models

### Text A — nature inspiration

> This design is inspired by leaves and flowers. The color palette is green, white, and soft pink. I chose a flowy fabric because it creates a soft and natural mood. The dress has a loose silhouette and small floral embroidery near the neckline. The overall look is calm and romantic.

### Text B — city inspiration

> This look is inspired by the city. The color palette is black, gray, and white. I chose straight lines and a structured jacket because they look modern. The jacket has a front zipper and large pockets. The overall look is practical, urban, and bold.

> This sketch is inspired by music and movement. The color palette is bright, with yellow and orange. I chose wide pants because they show movement. The top is cropped and simple. The main detail is a drawstring at the waist. The overall look is playful and energetic.

# Week 12 course-manual text summary

**Week 12 introduces creative inspiration and simple design-rationale language. Students learn to name inspiration sources such as nature, city, street style, music, art, architecture, culture, history, memory, and movement. They also learn mood and effect vocabulary such as calm, bold, playful, elegant, modern, romantic, strong, soft, energetic, contrast, movement, and balance. The online work includes inspiration-source vocabulary, mood/effect practice, reading model inspiration notes, listening to spoken design rationales, connecting inspiration to design elements, phrase-frame practice, guided writing, and targeted revision. The central language focus is “This design is inspired by…,” “The colors come from…,” “I chose ___ because…,” “This creates…,” and “The overall look is…”. Students produce a Design Inspiration Note that explains the source, visual choices, mood, and final impression of a look or sketch. The teacher-led session reinforces pronunciation, visual reasoning, and a short supported explanation of the student’s inspiration and design choices.**
