## Week 1 purpose

Week 1 establishes the routine for the whole course. The student learns how the ESP course works, completes a light diagnostic, practices basic fashion-student identity language, and produces a first reusable English artifact: a short profile saying who they are, what they study, and what areas of fashion/design interest them.

The week should feel welcoming, visual, low-pressure, and clearly connected to their degree. It introduces the platform rhythm:

1. visual opener
    
2. listening
    
3. reading / visual reading
    
4. vocabulary / phrases
    
5. writing
    
6. feedback / correction review
    
7. teacher-led progress session
    

## Week 1 learning outcomes

|Outcome type|By the end of Week 1, the student can…|
|---|---|
|Identity language|say they are a fashion design student in simple English|
|Course language|recognize basic words connected to the app/course routine: listen, read, write, submit, review, feedback|
|Fashion vocabulary|recognize first-set design-student words: fashion, design, sketch, fabric, sewing, patternmaking, collection, studio, portfolio|
|Reading|understand a short model profile of a fashion design student|
|Listening|understand a short introduction from a teacher or student about studying fashion design|
|Writing|write a 50–80 word guided fashion-student profile|
|Feedback routine|read simple correction feedback and make one small revision|
|Teacher session|introduce themselves orally using a prepared frame|

### Reading text model

Codex can generate a 90–110 word A2-level text like this:

> Ana is a fashion design student. She studies at university in Brazil. This semester, she has classes in drawing, sewing, patternmaking, creativity, art history, and text production. Ana likes sketching garments and choosing colors. She is interested in sustainable fashion and accessories. Her English is basic, but she wants to learn fashion vocabulary. She also wants to write short portfolio texts in English. In this course, Ana will practice listening, reading, vocabulary, writing, and correction review every week.

### Comprehension questions

|Question type|Example|
|---|---|
|multiple choice|What does Ana study?|
|true/false|Ana is interested in sustainable fashion.|
|multi-select|Which classes are mentioned?|
|matching|Match “sketching” → “drawing ideas for clothes”|
|short answer, optional|What does Ana want to learn?|

### Expected answer targets

The questions should check meaning, not trick the student. Main correct answers:

- Ana studies fashion design.
    
- She studies at university in Brazil.
    
- She has classes in drawing, sewing, patternmaking, creativity, art history, and text production.
    
- She likes sketching garments and choosing colors.
    
- She wants to learn fashion vocabulary and write short portfolio texts.

## Week 1 course-manual text summary

**Week 1 introduces students to English as a practical tool for fashion design study. Students learn the weekly rhythm of the course, practice basic fashion-student identity language, complete a light diagnostic, read and listen to model student introductions, learn a starter vocabulary set, and write their first short profile in English. The week connects directly to first-semester foundation coursework by using words from drawing, sewing, patternmaking, creativity, art history, and text production. The teacher-led session reinforces pronunciation, helps students say their profile aloud, and confirms that students understand how to complete tasks and review feedback.**
