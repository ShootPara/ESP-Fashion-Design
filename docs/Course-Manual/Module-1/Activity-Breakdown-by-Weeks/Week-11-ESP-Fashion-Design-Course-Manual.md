# Module 1 — Week 11 Detailed Breakdown

## Week 11 title

**Design Details**

## Position in module

|Field|Value|
|---|---|
|Module|Studio English Foundations I|
|Week|11 of 14|
|Student level|A2 English|
|Main university-course connection|Drawing, introductory sewing, introductory patternmaking, creativity lab, early design critique|
|Main ESP function|Describing visible design details, closures, trims, finishes, and decorative/functional features|
|Main student artifact|Design Detail Caption Set|
|App workload|75–90 minutes|
|Teacher-led session|45–60 minutes|

## Week 11 purpose

Week 11 narrows the focus after the broader Week 10 sketch description. Students now look closely at **design details**: the smaller visible features that make a garment specific, functional, decorative, or expressive.

This week is useful for fashion students because details appear everywhere in their coursework: sketch labels, sewing notes, pattern pieces, garment observation, moodboards, portfolio captions, visual critique, and product descriptions.

The main output is a **Design Detail Caption Set**: 3–5 short captions describing selected details from a garment or sketch. Each caption identifies the detail, says where it is, and explains whether it is functional, decorative, or both.

# Week 11 learning outcomes

|Outcome type|By the end of Week 11, the student can…|
|---|---|
|Detail vocabulary|recognize and name common garment design details|
|Function language|describe a detail as functional, decorative, practical, visible, hidden, simple, or expressive|
|Visual reading|identify details in close-up garment images or sketches|
|Listening|understand short spoken descriptions of garment details|
|Reading|understand short captions describing design details|
|Writing|write 3–5 short design-detail captions|
|Grammar / phrases|use “The main detail is…,” “There is/are…,” “It is placed…,” “It is functional/decorative because…”|
|Feedback routine|revise captions for clarity, articles, plural nouns, and location phrases|
|Teacher session|explain one garment detail using prepared frames|

# Main grammar / phrase targets

|Function|Sentence frames|
|---|---|
|Naming the detail|The main detail is a zipper.|
|Identifying location|The zipper is on the front.|
|Describing visible details|There are buttons on the sleeve.|
|Describing purpose|The zipper is functional.|
|Explaining function|It opens and closes the jacket.|
|Explaining decoration|It adds contrast. / It adds texture.|
|Saying both|It is functional and decorative.|
|Describing placement|The pocket is placed on the side.|
|Describing design effect|The ruffle makes the dress playful.|
|Simple opinion|I like this detail because it is simple and useful.|

## Language notes for Codex

|Language point|Instructional note|
|---|---|
|“detail”|Main noun for the week; use “design detail” for fashion context|
|“main detail”|Helps students identify one focus feature|
|“There is / There are”|Useful for captions: “There are buttons on the front”|
|“is placed”|Useful passive-like phrase for location without teaching full passive voice deeply|
|“functional/decorative”|Central contrast for the week|
|“adds”|Simple design-effect verb: “It adds color,” “It adds texture,” “It adds contrast”|
|“makes”|Simple effect phrase: “It makes the jacket more modern”|
|singular/plural|button/buttons, pocket/pockets, stripe/stripes|
|count/uncount|embroidery and topstitching can be treated as uncountable in simple captions|

|Required assets|student-selected garment/sketch with visible details from gallery or teacher-provided image|

## Writing prompt

**Portuguese student instruction:**

> Escolha uma peça de roupa ou um sketch da galeria. Escreva de 3 a 5 captions curtas em inglês para os detalhes de design. Para cada detalhe, diga o que é, onde está e se ele é funcional, decorativo ou os dois.

## Required content

|Required element|Sentence frame|
|---|---|
|detail name|The main detail is ___. / This detail is ___.|
|location|It is on the front/back/side. / It is near the ___.|
|role|It is functional/decorative.|
|effect|It adds color/texture/contrast.|
|reason|It is useful because ___. / It is decorative because ___.|
|opinion|I like this detail because ___.|

## Model answer — caption set

> **Caption 1:** The main detail is a zipper on the front of the jacket. It is functional because it opens and closes the garment.  
> **Caption 2:** There are two patch pockets on the front. They are practical and decorative.  
> **Caption 3:** The topstitching is visible near the seams. It adds contrast and makes the jacket look modern.  
> **Caption 4:** The collar is simple and structured. I like this detail because it makes the jacket look classic.

## Alternative model — dress details

> **Caption 1:** This detail is floral embroidery near the neckline. It is decorative because it adds color and texture.  
> **Caption 2:** The dress has a side zipper. It is functional because it helps open and close the garment.  
> **Caption 3:** The hem has a soft ruffle. It makes the dress playful and delicate.

|Priority|Feedback focus|

|1|correct detail vocabulary|
|2|clear location phrase|
|3|correct use of functional/decorative|
|4|clear use of “adds” or “makes”|
|5|correct singular/plural nouns|
|6|article use: a zipper, a pocket, the neckline|
|7|short caption clarity|
|8|spelling of design-detail words|

# Week 11 course-manual text summary

**Week 11 focuses on design details. Students learn to identify and describe visible garment features such as closures, pockets, ruffles, pleats, embroidery, prints, trims, bows, appliqués, topstitching, panels, slits, and color blocks. The week introduces the functional/decorative distinction and gives students practical sentence frames for short captions: “The main detail is…,” “It is placed…,” “It is functional because…,” “It is decorative because…,” “It adds…,” and “It makes the look…”. The online work includes vocabulary practice, functional/decorative sorting, visual scavenger hunting, detail-caption reading, listening identification, phrase-frame practice, guided caption writing, and targeted revision. Students produce a Design Detail Caption Set with 3–5 short captions. The teacher-led session reinforces pronunciation, close observation, and a short spoken explanation of one selected design detail.**
